# Invasion-beta

Liberation-style dynamic mission template for Arma 3. The `core.liberation` folder contains scripts and configs that auto-generate sectors on any map, populate them with configurable defenders, deliver a 70+ mission catalog, and provide a FOB-gated logistics build menu with resource/fuel/manpower economy and civilian workforce. Use the `mod_template` subfolder to mirror Liberation RX conventions and the `core.liberation/factions` directory (organized by `blufor`, `opfor`, and `independent`) to point mission parameters at ready-made presets (vanilla NATO/CSAT/AAF/Syndikat plus CTRG, RHS US/VDV/MSV, BAF variants, and CUP/3CB examples). See `TESTING.md` for manual validation steps inside Arma 3.

Each player also maintains private cash for town markets while the faction still shares the global resources/fuel pool for FOB logistics. Markets spawn shopkeepers and map markers in civilian settlements; players can browse weapon/gear/vehicle tabs, spend their own balance, and collect purchases spawned outside the stall. Mission completions and sector captures add to a per-player hourly income bonus so long-running campaigns reward active squads.

Server-hosted games automatically save progress to the profile every 15 minutes and on demand via the persistence helpers so captured sectors, FOBs, and resources survive restarts. A live battle overlay (configurable refresh interval) paints hotspots on the map and a buildable Tactical Map board at FOBs shows friendly fronts plus spotted enemies for on-base planning. Sector capture is kill-to-clear for BLUFOR: enemies (ignoring surrendered troops) must be wiped from the radius before ownership flips and entering players see a friendlies/enemies counter for situational awareness. OPFOR must hold a friendly-owned sector without any BLUFOR presence for a configurable window (5–20 minutes) before reclaiming it. FOB HQs are now mobile trucks that can be driven and deployed into static HQs; the mission seeds an initial deployed HQ at a safe distance from enemy sectors so players have a starting anchor before building more.

On terrains with meaningful coastlines or harbors the mission auto-enables naval fronts: ports and static carrier groups become captureable sectors guarded by boats and garrisons, both factions run shoreline patrols, and amphibious/naval assaults can open new flanks via beach landings. Captured ports track ownership for FOB logistics while carriers count as mobile air staging points once secured.

The scenario uses a vanilla Arma 3 loading-screen asset referenced directly from `description.ext`, keeping the mission free of custom binary artwork so it is easier to review and import.

AI groups scale their strength with the theater’s alert level and the new “Overall AI Presence” lobby parameter (50%–500%), flank and suppress under fire, and fall back to nearby buildings when mauled. They also have a small but rising chance to surrender when wounded, outnumbered, or heavily attrited—captives switch to captive state so POW handling and exchanges still work. Ambient suicide and grenade-dropping drones now roam the front lines for both factions, harassing targets around contested sectors to make the war feel more lethal. Both factions can commit occasional atrocities that are flagged on the map; squads must detain friendly offenders at a Friendly Prison buildable and spend resources to pardon them, while enemy war criminals and POWs are held in a separate Prison structure for swaps. Players who violate ROE are likewise marked, hunted by nearby friendlies, and must be escorted to a Friendly Prison before being released for a steep cost.

The live overlay now draws BLUFOR and OPFOR forward positions with a dynamically shifting no-man’s-land between them; several recon- and movement-oriented side missions seed around that buffer. Artillery, mortar, and bomber threats broadcast incoming alerts—FOB buildings at risk can be repaired via resource spend after a barrage—and frontline ambushes or nighttime special-operations raids add more unpredictable contact. Medical tents at FOBs enable stabilizing wounded enemies for POW processing, and the campaign now ends cleanly when every sector is secured (win) or when all FOB anchors are destroyed (loss).
